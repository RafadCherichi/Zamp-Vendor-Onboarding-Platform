# Vendor onboarding: a case a buyer can actually decide

Decision brief for review · 9 November 2026 · Proposed work, not a release report

**Recommendation:** Build one reliable journey first: a reviewer sees a specific problem, requests a specific piece of evidence, the vendor replies, and the same reviewer sees what changed and can make the right decision. Build each dashboard around its part of that journey. Use the existing free deployment, document processing and run history.

**Accepted direction:** Proceed with this roadmap and make the staff portals noticeably more graphic and animated. The visual work should help people understand workload and progress at a glance; it must not hide evidence, delay a decision, or make motion the only way to read a state.

## Evidence and scope

The [candidate guide](./case-study/candidate-guide.pdf) asks PS-2 to take a realistic vendor submission, assess completeness, consistency and credibility, and produce an explained decision. It requires an actual live run, history and outputs, two to four deliberate edge cases, and a five-minute demo including a happy path and an edge case. It does not prescribe a staffing model. The consolidated local product specification explicitly includes assigning reviewers among procurement duties.

I reviewed the four supplied screenshots (the first two show the same receipt), the supplied registry output, the application and exception workflows, and the published design references below. A read-only check on the hosted demo confirmed that APP-2026-PROP-001 was under review, with a resolved potential-duplicate exception and a current duplicate-check blocker. No live decision was changed. The specific missing vendor reply has not been independently replayed; its storage, delivery and presentation need separate checks.

## Implementation checkpoint · 9 November 2026

The approved direction is now being applied in the working tree. The first implementation slice includes:

- Demo autofill shows only the signed-in seed vendor's own company and matching synthetic documents. It no longer lets the Apex vendor account populate Tata, Sharma or LLP identities.
- Vendor receipt content shares one left reading edge. Approval remains on the existing application page, states what the review approval covers, explains that payment or purchasing setup may be separate, and accurately says portal updates are available while email updates are not.
- While a vendor application is awaiting a decision, an open portal tab refreshes on focus and periodically, so a persisted approval or clarification is reflected without requiring a new sign-in.
- Notification entries now open their application. Exception notices open that application at the Exceptions section.
- The reviewer, procurement and admin homes now use a forest/ochre or warm-paper workflow panel with live counts, clickable stages, staggered entrance motion and hover feedback. Motion is brief and removed with reduced-motion settings; values come from persisted metrics.
- The queue uses the same approval eligibility check as the decision endpoint for “Ready to decide”. It shows the current blocker even when there are zero open exceptions, and it labels an outstanding vendor response for reviewer attention.
- General reviewer clarifications now create a durable vendor response task. The reviewer can optionally select the precise document slot; the vendor's replacement is uploaded to that slot, and notification navigation opens the case at its Exceptions section.
- Reviewer assignment is available to procurement officers and administrators. The backend rejects reviewer-role assignment writes; administrators must record a reason for ownership changes, and reviewers can still see who owns the case.
- Manual dispositions for failed validation findings must link to the finding/check and include a rationale. Vendor corrections do not clear a still-failed finding until a fresh check passes.
- The registry view now uses plain result language, masks identifiers, identifies sample sources, and places reference details behind a disclosure instead of presenting raw JSON as the main view.
- Health diagnostics no longer appear on the business dashboards. The admin home is rewritten around case ownership, escalations and decision history instead of tenant IDs and implementation details.

The frontend production build, lint command, Python syntax compilation, diff whitespace check, and all 119 backend tests pass against a disposable database created from the current migrations and demo seed. The lint command reports existing React hook / Fast Refresh warnings; the production build also reports the existing single JavaScript chunk over 500 kB. This checkpoint has not yet had a browser visual review at mobile sizes, a recorded end-to-end hosted run, or a production deployment. Those remain required before claiming the redesign is finished or regrading it.

## Decisions on the 15 observations

**Approval notice check:** the approval service creates a persisted in-app notification for the vendor account and changes application status. The vendor sees an approval screen after the application is fetched again. The notification bell refreshes the unread count every 30 seconds, but the notification list loads only when opened; notification entries are not links. There is no email sender in this repository. The approval receipt says the procurement representative “will contact you”, but no automatic procurement follow-up task is created by approval. The status timeline also says “Approved & active on vendor panel”; the code does not establish that payment onboarding or a vendor master record has been completed. So there is a basic in-app signal, but no assured timely notice or defined next step. Do not claim email delivery.

## What the reviewer actually sees

For the reported Tata case the first screen should say:

> **Tata Industrial Supplies Private Limited** · Under review · Assigned to Rajesh Iyer
>
> **Possible duplicate supplier** — This application matches an existing vendor record. The earlier task is closed, but the match still needs a documented decision.
> **Compare vendors** · View submitted documents

Show the two application references, masked matching identifiers, differences and evidence versions side by side. The comparison must use actual matched fields; do not claim two suppliers share an account number merely because their names match. After a valid disposition, replace the action with the next blocker, or **Ready for your decision**. “Approved” is shown only after a person records approval.

For a correction: **Sharma Engineering Works · Vendor replied · Review replacement cheque**. One click should show the original question, the vendor's answer, the old and new masked details, and the latest check. The reviewer can accept the correction, ask a precise follow-up, or escalate. While a check is running, show that state; a successful upload alone does not mean the mismatch is fixed.

**Approval notification rule:** the application status is the source of truth; an in-app notification and an optional configured email are delivery channels. Make notification creation part of the same committed decision transaction (or a durable outbox), target every appropriate account contact without duplicating notices, and record delivery attempts. Opening the notification routes to the application. The vendor receipt remains correct after refresh and sign-in. If email is not configured, no message should imply it was sent.

## Approval and the supplier's next visit

I checked the actual product path rather than inferring it from the screenshot. Approval changes the existing application to `APPROVED`, records reviewer/time, and creates an in-app `STATUS_CHANGE` notice for the account that submitted it. There is no email integration. `VendorDashboard` routes approved applications to `SubmissionConfirmation`; it keeps the same receipt and progress page. It does not redirect to another dashboard. The latest implementation refreshes a pending vendor page on focus and every 30 seconds, and the receipt says the review is approved, payment/purchasing setup may be separate, and email updates are unavailable. There is still no buyer-specific follow-up task, payment activation status, or supplier task center.

This means the user **is informed in the portal after refresh, focus, or the next periodic check**, and not through email. The in-app notice deep-links to the application. Keep the bookmarked application page as the durable source and only add a new supplier home if the buyer provides real post-onboarding work there.

### Questions the approval experience must answer

1. **Was my application approved, and by whom/when?** Show the decision, buyer name, timestamp, and existing reference.
2. **What exactly does approval cover?** State that onboarding review is approved; avoid implying the company has been added to a payment system, can receive purchase orders, or is already payable unless that integration/state exists.
3. **Do I need to do anything?** Say “Nothing needed from you” only if true. If procurement still needs to establish a supplier record or payment profile, show the owner, current status, and what the vendor must supply, if anything.
4. **How will I know if that next step changes?** Link to the in-app application page and update it after the status changes; offer email only when a working email channel is configured.
5. **Where do I return later?** Keep the existing authenticated application page/reference as a receipt. If future supplier tasks exist (profile changes, purchase orders, invoices, contacts), then make the landing page a real Supplier home that links to those functions; otherwise the receipt remains the right destination.
6. **Who do I contact if the next step stalls?** Show the buyer's named contact or a monitored procurement contact route, not an unowned generic message.

The vendor portal now answers questions 1 and 2, and partly answers 4 and 5: it shows the decision/reference and its scope, refreshes after approval, and links the in-app notice to the record. There is no email channel, buyer contact, tracked supplier-master/payment status or actual post-approval task list, so questions 3, 4 and 6 remain conditional on the buyer's real next step. Do not claim email delivery solely because a database notice was created.

## Ownership policy to implement

| Action | Vendor | Procurement | Assigned reviewer | Administrator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Submit / answer | Own invitation and requested evidence | Track progress | Inspect response | Audit access as authorized |
| Route a case | — | Assign active eligible reviewer in same organization | Request transfer; no peer reassignment | Configure eligible pool; reassign with reason |
| Clarify / follow up | Reply to request | Chase outstanding request; coordinate intake | Specify evidence needed; assess response | Handle escalations or arrange cover |
| Decide | View reason/outcome | View status | Decide when permitted; record reason | Govern policy; decision only if explicitly authorized and assigned |

This is a proposed operating model, not a PS-2 rule. Admin-only routing gives one person control but makes every submission wait for them. Unrestricted peer reassignment makes accountability hard to follow. Delegated procurement routing matches the product specification. Persist **current owner**, **party who must act next**, and **person who made the final decision** separately. Enforce membership, role, ownership and organization on every write, including direct requests; log old/new owner and actor. Two simultaneous assignments or decisions must not both succeed.

### Visual system proposal

- **Reviewer home:** a calm ivory canvas with a forest green masthead, one small animated queue-flow ribbon showing where cases are waiting, and a large “Your next decisions” stack. A flagged case gets a distinctive evidence-led panel with issue glyph, age, owner and action. Use a compact animated count only when its source data changes; never use decorative counters.
- **Procurement home:** a visual pipeline from invited → started → submitted → in review → complete, with counts and aging bands. Click a stage to open its exact cohort. Highlight stale handoffs in amber and offer **Invite supplier** as the main action.
- **Admin home:** a quiet governance studio with reviewer capacity, unassigned work and escalations; no infrastructure diagrams or live database stats. Use small motion to show workload shifts and ownership changes.
- **Case workspace:** a clear first-screen decision story: identity, owner, issue, proof comparison and next choice. Let background texture, custom line illustrations and small motion add character; evidence tables and sensitive values remain still and easy to scan.
- **Motion rules:** 120–220 ms for small interface changes; one brief 300–500 ms reveal for a new case/reply; no looping animation, no motion on sensitive values, no auto-scrolling. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, provide visible focus, and keep status announcements in text. `prefers-reduced-motion` removes nonessential movement without removing feedback.

**Art direction call:** editorial operations journal rather than generic SaaS cards: oversized but readable case numbers, abstract route maps / linked evidence motifs, warm natural surfaces, intentional asymmetry only in decorative regions, and aligned controls. Keep the familiar Zamp wordmark and current forest/ochre palette as anchors. Build a reference screen in code, capture it at laptop and phone sizes, and then apply the approved tokens across reviewer, procurement and admin surfaces. Do not introduce a second visual language into the vendor receipt.

**Scope trade-off:** Ship steps 1–5 and a minimal usable reviewer/procurement view before investing in admin charts, configurable workflow builders or new registry integrations. The receipt/tag patch can proceed in parallel. Keep support-only diagnostics off the critical path. Roll out against disposable cases first; compare old and new decision results on the same fixtures before switching enforcement. Roll back presentation independently if needed; never roll back by disabling the approval checks.

## Measures for review

Use the existing rubric weights: working process 24, decision safety 20, edge cases 16, live visibility 15, run dashboard 10, UI/language 8, judgment 4, demo 3. The live duplicate case puts A4, B1/B4 and E3 in question; the screenshots give evidence against full F1 credit. Do not declare a new numerical score without rerunning the cases.

Proposed release targets, **not measured results**: zero unsafe approvals on labelled blocking cases; zero cross-account fixture changes in permission tests; 100% agreement between queue, case and decision service on the acceptance set; each requested reply accessible in one action from the reviewer row; owned open dashboards reflect a submitted reply within 15 seconds under normal connectivity. Verify stale/offline state separately. A useful target for first-time task tests: at least four of five representative users find the blocker and next action unaided in 30 seconds. Report individual failures and sample size; five participants cannot establish production rates.

For a four-week pilot, collect median submit-to-decision time, vendor-response-to-review time, repeat requests for the same proof, and count/age of open cases. Segment by case type. Pair speed with incorrect approvals and incorrect continuing blocks. This shows whether work got easier without rewarding rushed approval or ignoring difficult cases.

## Research behind the choices

- **Return for evidence, then review again:** Oracle distinguishes registration approval, rejection with reasons, requests for information and returning the supplier's saved registration for resubmission. Its flow supports a reason and a link back to the request. We adapt this to a narrower field or proof request. [Oracle: supplier registration approval actions](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/procurement/26b/oaprc/supplier-registration-approval-actions.html)
- **Approval may be only one stage of supplier activation:** Oracle separates registration approval and Supplier Master creation from a possible additional spend-authorization process; its documentation also warns that missing supplier bank-account setup can prevent electronic payment. For this prototype, use those examples to ask what the buyer's actual downstream step is, not to copy Oracle's workflow as a requirement. [Oracle: supplier creation after registration approval](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/procurement/26d/oaprc/supplier-creation-post-registration-request-approval.html) · [Oracle: supplier registration process](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/procurement/25d/oaprc/supplier-registration-process.html) · [Oracle: bank-account setup](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/procurement/25c/oaprc/set-up-a-supplier-s-bank-account.html)
- **Separate routing from authority:** Oracle describes role-based approval routing in supplier registration. OWASP recommends least privilege and verifying access on each request. These support an eligible reviewer pool and server-side checks; our exact procurement/admin split is a local product decision. [Oracle: supplier registration process](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/procurement/26d/oaprc/supplier-registration-process.html) · [OWASP: authorization](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html)
- **Give the vendor closure:** GOV.UK's confirmation pattern pairs a receipt/reference with what happens next. Its tag pattern distinguishes a status label from an interactive action. The single left edge and responsive row design are our application of those principles to the screenshots. [Confirmation pages](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/confirmation-pages/) · [Status tags](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/tag/)
- **Design notifications around an action:** IBM's Carbon guidelines distinguish actionable notifications and their destinations. The application already knows the recipient and subject in many events; the design should bring people back to the relevant work. [Carbon: notifications](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/notification/usage/)
- **Verify the compressed layout:** W3C's reflow guidance explains how content should remain usable in narrow viewports. For this queue, preserving vendor, issue and action in a stacked layout is preferable to squeezing seven columns. [W3C: understanding reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html)
