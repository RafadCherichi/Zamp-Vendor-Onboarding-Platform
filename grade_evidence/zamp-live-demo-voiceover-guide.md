# Zamp live demo — human voiceover guide

This is a real browser recording using synthetic practice data. The spoken guide is optional. Read the paragraphs below in a natural conversational voice, or send a recording and the editor can align it with the footage. Each heading gives the approximate start time.

## 00:00 — Supplier sign-in

I'm starting at the supplier sign-in page. This is Vikram's account for Apex Components. The sample details and documents are synthetic, while the submission and decisions happen in the working product.

## 00:17 — Clean application submitted live

Vikram can review his company's details, the required documents, and the declarations before he submits. I'm using the account's matching sample data to keep the demo short. Now I move to the review step and submit. Watch the document-reading and checks run. Once they're done, the supplier gets a reference and can see that a person will review the result. The application doesn't disappear into a black box.

## 00:51 — Procurement assigns ownership

Next, I'll sign in as Pooja from procurement. Her job is to keep the queue moving. She can see the new supplier, what needs attention, and who owns the next step. I'll assign this case to Rajesh. That way it reaches the right reviewer, and the handoff is recorded.

## 01:16 — Reviewer checks and decides

Now I sign in as Rajesh, the assigned reviewer. Before making a decision, he can open the supplier's documents and see what the automated checks found. I'll show both. The checks are consistent for this clean case, but they don't approve a supplier by themselves. Rajesh makes that decision, records a reason, and approves. If a serious issue were still open, this approval button would be blocked.

## 01:55 — Supplier receives the decision

Back in Vikram's account, the dashboard says approved. A notification opens the decision receipt: the review is complete and there are no remaining tasks. The supplier gets a clear answer instead of waiting on a submitted status.

## 02:15 — Second supplier signs in

Now for a harder case. I'm signing in as a second practice supplier in another buyer workspace. This draft has never been checked; its submission is live.

## 02:30 — GST identity conflict submitted live

Look at these two numbers. The company says this is its PAN. The GST number looks valid too, but the PAN inside that GST number belongs to a different entity. A format check alone would miss that. I'll submit the draft now. Zamp runs the checks and flags this exact conflict. Approval is held, and the supplier is told to confirm which entity's credentials are correct. Notice that this is an identity mismatch, not a duplicate supplier in the buyer's database.

## 03:13 — Reviewer sees the blocker

Procurement assigns this new case, and I open it as the reviewer. The approval action is disabled. The issue card explains the mismatch in ordinary language and tells the supplier what to correct. A reviewer can follow the evidence without searching through system logs.

## 03:39 — Supplier responds; reviewer sees it

I'll return to the supplier account and enter the GST number that matches the declared PAN, with a short explanation. Sending that response starts another check. When I switch back to the reviewer, the queue marks the case as having a supplier reply, and the correction is right on the case. It still needs a human decision.

## 04:12 — Saved history and uncertainty

Processing Runs keeps the original failed finding. Another prepared case shows a sample registry timeout. That is unknown evidence, not proof the supplier is wrong; staff should retry or review it. Documents and registry replies are synthetic. In a pilot, I'd measure decision time, unsafe approvals and valid suppliers held unnecessarily.
